a table of functions and their Laplace transform
$$
\begin{array}{llc}
\textbf{Type} & f(t) \,\, (t > 0^-) & F(s) \\
\hline

\text{(ramp)} & t & \dfrac{1}{s^2} \\[1px]
\text{(exponential)} & e^{-at} & \dfrac{1}{s + a} \\[1px]
\text{(sine)} & \sin \omega t & \dfrac{\omega}{s^2 + \omega^2} \\[1px]
\text{(cosine)} & \cos \omega t & \dfrac{s}{s^2 + \omega^2} \\[1px]
\text{(damped ramp)} & t e^{-at} & \dfrac{1}{(s + a)^2} \\[1px]
\text{(damped sine)} & e^{-at} \sin \omega t & \dfrac{\omega}{(s + a)^2 + \omega^2} \\[1px]
\text{(damped cosine)} & e^{-at} \cos \omega t & \dfrac{s + a}{(s + a)^2 + \omega^2} \\
\end{array}
$$

## quick characteristics revision

constants don't change
$$
\text{L}\{K\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = K\color{#C210C2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2}
$$

since it's an integration it can be factored
$$
\text{L}\{\color{#00CCCC}f_1(t)\color{#F8EFD2} + \color{#00CCCC}f_2(t)\color{#F8EFD2} - \color{#00CCCC}f_3(t)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = \color{#C210C2}F_1(s)\color{#F8EFD2} + \color{#C210C2}F_2(s)\color{#F8EFD2} - \color{#C210C2}F_3(s)\color{#F8EFD2}
$$

the transformation for the derivative
$$
\text{L}\left\{\frac{d\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}}{dt}\right\} = s\color{#C210C2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2} - \color{#00CCCC}f(0^-)\color{#F8EFD2}
$$

$$
\text{L}\left\{\frac{d^n\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}}{dt^n}\right\} = s^n\color{#C210C2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2} - s^{n-1}\color{#00CCCC}f(0^-)\color{#F8EFD2} - s^{n-2}\frac{d\color{#00CCCC}f(0^-)\color{#F8EFD2}}{dt}- \dots - \frac{d^{n-1}\color{#00CCCC}f(0^-)\color{#F8EFD2}}{dt^{n-1}}
$$

the transformation for the integral
$$
\text{L}\left\{\int_{0^-}^{t} \color{#00CCCC}f(x)\color{#F8EFD2} \, dx\right\} = \frac{\color{#C210C2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2}}{s}
$$

$$
\text{L}\{\color{#00CCCC}f(t - a)\color{#F8EFD2}\color{#00CCCC}u(t - a)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = e^{-as}\color{#C210C2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2}
$$
multiplying by an exponential corresponds to a *shift* in the input
$$
\text{L}\{e^{-at}\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = \color{#C210C2}F(s + a)\color{#F8EFD2}
$$

$$
\text{L}\{\color{#00CCCC}f(at)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = \frac{1}{a}\color{#C210C2}F\left(\frac{s}{a}\right)\color{#F8EFD2}, \quad a > 0
$$

notice how multiplying by $t$ corresponds to differentiation or integration:

$$
\text{L}\{t\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = -\frac{d\color{#B200B2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2}}{ds}
$$

$$
\text{L}\{t^n\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}\} = (-1)^n \frac{d^n \color{#B200B2}F(s)\color{#F8EFD2}}{ds^n}
$$

$$
\text{L}\left\{\frac{\color{#00CCCC}f(t)\color{#F8EFD2}}{t}\right\} = \int_{s}^{\infty} \color{#B200B2}F(u)\color{#F8EFD2} \, du
$$

# Poles and Zeros of F(s)

for a function
$$
F(S) = \frac{N(s)}{D(s)}
$$
**Zeros** result when N(s) = 0
**Poles** result when D(s) = 0


# Circuit Elements in the s Domain
## sources

when transferring voltage and current voltage, if they're constant they're transformed as a step function
$$
K \cdot u(t) = \frac{K}{s}
$$
where $K$ is the source's output value

if it's not a constant value *-rare-* you'll have to deal with it as a function and get the Laplace transform it

## Resistor

$$
v = Ri \quad\rightarrow\quad V = RI
$$
## Inductor

$$
V = sLI - LI_0 \quad,\quad I = \frac{V}{sL} + \frac{I_0}{s}
$$
the term $I_0$ is the starting current value in the inductor, equals zero if the current wasn't charged

the inductor will have an impedance of $sL$

## Capacitor

$$
V = \frac{I}{sC} + \frac{V_0}{s} \quad,\quad I = sCV - CV_0
$$
same as the inductor, $V_0$ is the starting voltage value in the capacitor

the capacitor will have an impedance of $1/{sC}$
