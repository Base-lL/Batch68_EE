# The gradient process for $\vec E = -\vec\nabla V$ 
The gradient for $\vec\nabla V$ for each coordinate
## Cartesian
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial x} \vec{a_x} +\frac{\partial V}{\partial y} \vec{a_y} +\frac{\partial V}{\partial z} \vec{a_z}
$$
## Cylindrical
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial \rho} \vec{a_\rho} + \frac{1}{\rho}  \frac{\partial V}{\partial \phi} \vec{a_\phi} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial z} \vec{a_z}
$$
## Spherical
$$
\frac{\partial V}{\partial r} \vec{a_r}+ \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial V}{\partial \theta} \vec{a_\theta}+ \frac{1}{r \sin\theta} \frac{\partial V}{\partial \phi} \vec{a_\phi}
$$
*notice* that $\vec\nabla V$ and $\vec\nabla \cdot V$ are *NOT THE SAME* , refer back to [[4. Divergence - application of Gauss law|the divergence section]]
# Dipole
## for 2 point charges that are
- equal in magnitude $Q$
- opposite in sign
- separated by a -very- small distance $d$
### the potential difference at a point $P$ is: 
$$
\begin{array}{l}
V &= V_{+Q} + V_{-Q} \\[3px]
&= \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0\, R_1} + \dfrac{-Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0\, R_2} \\[3px]
&= \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{1}{R_1} - \dfrac{1}{R_2} \right)\\[3px]
&= \dfrac{Q}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\left(\dfrac{R_2 - R_1}{R_1 R_2} \right)\\[3px]
\end{array}
$$
### where $R_1\;,\;R_2$ are the distance between P and each point
#### since
$$
\begin{array}{l}
		R \gg d \quad\Rightarrow\quad R_2 - R_1 = d\cos\theta\\	R_1 \simeq R_2 \simeq R \quad\Rightarrow\quad R_1 R_2 \simeq R^2 \\[2.5px]
\end{array}
$$
#### then
$$
\begin{array}{l}
	 V=\dfrac{Q\,d\cos\theta}{4\pi\epsilon_0\,R^2}
\end{array}
$$
### the dipole moment: $\vec P = Q \vec d \quad [c\cdot m]$ 
$$
\large V=\dfrac{\vec P \cdot \vec{a_R} }{4\pi\epsilon_0\,R^2} + \dfrac{\vec P \cdot \vec{R} }{4\pi\epsilon_0\,R^3}
$$
### the electric field due to a dipole with center at the origin
$$
\large\vec E = \dfrac{ |P| }{4\pi\epsilon_0\,R^3} \left[ 2\cos\theta\;\vec{a_R} + \sin \theta\;\vec{a_\theta} \right]
$$
